What parda masks

Rules, not guesswork. When a rule is unsure, it masks: a false find costs the AI a little context and is restored for you anyway; a miss is a leak.

Secrets

  • API keys with a known shape: Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS, GitHub, GitLab, Slack (tokens and webhooks), Stripe, Google (API keys and OAuth tokens), Hugging Face, PyPI, npm, SendGrid, Twilio.
  • JWTs, bearer tokens, Basic auth, and private keys in PEM blocks — even when the END line was cut off.
  • Passwords inside URLs (postgres://app:this part@db) and in curl -u user:this part.
  • Values of settings whose name says secret, in .env files, shell commands, YAML, JSON and code: DB_PASSWORD=…, PGPASSWORD=… psql, "clientSecret": "…", api_key: …. Quoted passwords may contain spaces.

Settings named key, token or auth are only masked when the value looks random, so max_tokens: 4096, primary_key: id and auth: true are left alone. Names containing password, secret or credential are always masked. Code like token = getToken() and placeholders like ${API_KEY} are left alone.

Names

A name is two or more words. parda uses lists of given names, middle names and surnames (English, South Asian, Nepali and others) to decide:

  • English script: capitalised words that start with a known given name (John Doe) or end in a distinctive surname (Nirvik Shrestha, Priya Patel). Titles like Dr or Mr stay outside the mask.
  • Devanagari: a known surname with the word before it (सीता शर्मा, राम बहादुर थापा), or a known given name followed by middle names (हरि प्रसाद). Postpositions written onto the name are included: अधिकारीले.

Known limits

Better to know them than to discover them:

  • Single-word names ("ask Sita") and lower-case names are not masked. Too many ordinary words would be caught in code.
  • Names not on the lists, where neither the first nor the last word is known, are missed.
  • Addresses, dates of birth and free-text descriptions of a person are not detected.
  • Images, PDFs and audio inside a request are passed through without being checked.
  • Some ordinary words that are also surnames (for example अधिकारी, "officer") are masked when they follow another word. That's the "when unsure, mask" rule: the AI sees a stand-in and your answer has the real word back.

Found a miss or a false find? Report it with a made-up example — never paste the real data.
